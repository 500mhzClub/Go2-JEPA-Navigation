# Review, 11 November 2026: the path to a generic JEPA recipe for navigating a novel world

Asked for on 11 November, after P21's first run (R0) failed: re-review the whole plan against the literature, the
implementation and the stated goal, a generic recipe for training a JEPA that navigates a novel environment, and
give the strongest evidence-based path. Approved the same day ("All approved"), with the decisions in section 6.

## Bottom line

The evidence points away from what P20 and P21 were doing, training small JEPAs from scratch on this project's own
maze data, and towards the recipe the literature has converged on, which the earlier LeWMQuad-v3 project had reached
in August:

> frozen pretrained dense image features, then self-supervised object slots, then an action-conditioned predictor
> over the objects with object-level masking, then planning with this project's intrinsic cost, with the map and
> the router as memory.

Its two halves are tested apart, each where it is cheapest, in parallel: the dynamics of objects in the ring world
(P22), and slots on the Go2's camera in Genesis (P23). They are joined in Genesis after that (P24).

## 1. What the evidence says

**Small from-scratch JEPAs lose small objects that move on their own.** The most consistent finding, here and in
the literature.
- Zhu et al. 2026 (arXiv 2609.04264): LeWM, from scratch with one [CLS] vector, shows "physical representation
  laziness" in bouncing-ball worlds, planning 0-3% against 60-92% with object-aware targets (the targets are boxes:
  supervised).
- The LeWM paper (arXiv 2603.19312) has no task with objects the agent does not control and no test on unseen
  layouts; on its one navigation task (TwoRoom) PLDM and DINO-WM beat it.
- Here, P4 to P4f: the encoder holds the walker (0.13-0.15 m) and the predictor keeps about half its motion
  (velocity skill 0.21 on true states, 0.10 through rollouts) and never learns the reaction; freezing the project's
  own encoder did not help. Phase C: the latent was 89% command history. P21's R0 (the ring alone): walkers held in
  34% of cases 1.6 s ahead, 22% at 3.2 s; 30 of 80 round trips with walkers that do not yield and 44 of 80 with
  polite ones, against the ring tracker's 59-62 and 78.

**Frozen pretrained dense features with a learned action-conditioned predictor is what works for planning.**
- DINO-WM (arXiv 2411.04983): frozen DINOv2 patch features, zero-shot planning, mazes included.
- V-JEPA 2-AC (arXiv 2506.09985): a frozen video encoder and a predictor trained on under 62 hours of unlabelled
  robot video, deployed zero-shot in new labs.
- Terver et al. 2026 (arXiv 2512.24497): DINO image features beat V-JEPA video features for planning, DINOv2 best on
  synthetic navigation; patch tokens; a depth-6 AdaLN predictor; 3 frames of history; a 2-step training rollout
  beats longer ones in simulation; larger encoders do not help in simulation; proprioception helps; CEM with an L2
  cost. Their model beats DINO-WM and V-JEPA 2-AC (Maze 83.9 against 81.6, Wall 78.8 against 64.1). This contradicts
  two of P21's planned remedies: longer training rollouts and a larger predictor.
- LeWMQuad-v3, August: on the same occupancy probe, frozen V-JEPA 2.1 0.51 and DINOv2 0.47 against 0.37 for that
  project's own ViT; letting the encoder train reduced action discrimination.

**Object-level structure is what makes interactions learnable.**
- C-JEPA (arXiv 2602.11389; LeWM's group): masking whole objects in the history gives +21 points on counterfactual
  questions; it plans near DINO-WM (Push-T 88.7% against 91.3%) on 1% of the tokens, about 8.5 times faster. Its
  slots come from a frozen self-supervised encoder (VideoSAUR on frozen DINOv2); joint training is its future work.
- VideoSAUR (arXiv 2306.04829): slots bind through a motion-sensitive temporal-similarity target, and hold up on real
  video with camera motion.
- FactorJEPA (arXiv 2608.01049): factorising layout, agents and interactions helps, but its factors are supervised
  by pseudo-labels.

**Planning and memory.** PLDM (arXiv 2502.14819): planning on a learned latent model generalises to unseen layouts
better than model-free RL. Long horizons need a hierarchy (H-JEPA, arXiv 2610.06805: AntMaze 18% to 73%) or a
learned value (Destrade et al., arXiv 2601.00844). Here, P17: 6.4-s tapes with a walker-aware end cost reach about
the ceiling, and the map and router give the long horizon.

**The positive quadruped JEPA result is narrower than it looks.** The real-Go2 navigation paper the August review
cites feeds the policy explicit tracked obstacle states; prediction improved anticipation of their motion (from the
August review, not re-read here). It agrees with P20: the ring tracker's states carry nearly all that true velocity
is worth (59-62 against 64-65).

**Two known limits.** A deterministic predictor learns the mean of the futures (Terver et al.), and a walker at a
junction has several. The stand-off data were scripted from privileged state; P20's random-intervention corpus fixes
that, for the ring only.

## 2. What it means for the plan

| Current plan | Verdict | Change |
|---|---|---|
| A ring-scan JEPA from scratch (P21: R0 to R3, slots on the ring) | Every line above says it keeps losing walkers; no precedent for slots on a 1-D scan with ego-motion | Stopped |
| Longer training rollouts, a larger predictor | Contradicted by Terver et al. | Dropped |
| The from-scratch image LeWM as the walkers' model | Laziness, and nothing to carry to a novel world | Kept only as the robot's own-motion forecaster in missions until replaced |
| H-JEPA | Not needed while the router supplies the long horizon | Deferred (section 5) |
| The kinematic world, the ladder, the ring tracker, P20's corpus | Most useful things built | Kept, for P22 |
| P17's planner and cost, P16's measures, P19's tools, the harness | Still right | Kept |

## 3. The path

1. **P22: can a self-supervised object-level predictor learn walkers' motion and reactions, and plan better for
   it?** In the ring world, on the CPU. Object tokens from the ring tracker, a robot token, the walls around them; a
   C-JEPA-style action-conditioned predictor with object masking and a 2-step training rollout; future positions
   read from the predicted tokens. The core question in its cleanest form: no perception in the way, exact
   counterfactuals, a known ladder.
2. **P23: do self-supervised slots find and follow walkers from the Go2's moving camera?** In parallel, on the GPU,
   offline on corpus v2's frames: frozen DINOv2, VideoSAUR's slots, a slot placed by the ring's range in its
   direction. Beside it, the baseline the August review called mandatory: a DINO-WM-style predictor over every patch,
   scored on P16's measures.
3. **P24: joined in Genesis.** Data with random interventions; P22's predictor over P23's slots, with ring-tracker
   objects for walkers out of the camera's view; P17's planner in missions on the built map.
4. **P25: generality and the contribution.** Held-out maze families; MOVi as an outside check on the slots; a learned
   end cost; and whether adapting the encoder to the robot improves the dynamics without losing the pretrained
   geometry, the question the August review named as the contribution.

No simulator label enters a decision: positions come from the tracker (P22) and from slots with the ring's ranges
(P23, P24), engineered estimation from observations. Labels are used only in measures.

## 4. Risks

| Risk | Fallback |
|---|---|
| Slots do not bind to walkers while the robot moves (the largest) | Motion targets compensated by odometry, or the ring tracker's objects as the tokens: estimation engineered, dynamics learned |
| The camera's field of view misses walkers beside and behind | The ring tracker's objects |
| A walker's future has several possibilities and a deterministic predictor averages them | Sampled futures; P17's fresh-dice row (69) shows sampling works |
| Genesis data collection costs GPU-days | Sized before P24 |

## 5. Compatibility with H-JEPA

The recipe replaces level 1 of a hierarchy, and `models/hjepa.py` treats level 1 as a module. Upper levels take
level-1 states and macro-actions and plan top-down; object slots separate fast motion from slow facts (which corridor
a walker holds, how far along its route the robot is), the separated timescales H-JEPA needs, and are far cheaper to
stack on than patches. Two changes: upper levels need a set encoder over slots (or one coarse state per object), with
slot identity kept over time; and gradients from upper levels reach the slot module and the level-1 predictor, with
DINOv2 fixed below the hierarchy, keeping the slot module's own losses and SIGReg on the slot latents as anchors (no
paper has shown slots staying good under JEPA gradients). H-JEPA still needs a memory to take over routing (each upper
state is one frame's function; the 9 November review's finding), and phase C's upper levels became action codes, so
object masking and the action-share check go in at every level. Kept open now: the same predictor class and command
format as `hjepa.py`, recurrent slots, SIGReg on slot latents from the first run, a subgoal term in the level-1
planner beside the router's waypoint, and each level's action share tracked. Decided after P24.

## 6. Decisions (approved 11 November)

1. P21's ring-scan JEPA stops; P22 and P23 run in parallel.
2. The pretrained encoder is frozen first; adapting it to the robot is tested in P25 (the project's earlier rule,
   never freeze level 1, is kept by placing level 1 above the frozen DINOv2: the slot module and the predictor).
3. The ring tracker's objects stand in for slots in P22, as engineered estimation, and are replaced by slots in P24.
4. The H-JEPA choices of section 5 are built in.
