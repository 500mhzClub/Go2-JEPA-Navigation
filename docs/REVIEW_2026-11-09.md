# Review: does phase C test the thesis, and what comes next? (9 November 2026)

The goal: a robot that navigates with as few hand-written rules as possible, its behaviour coming from a
self-supervised world model and a planner. The review of 7 November (`docs/REVIEW_2026-11-07.md`) set phases A-E. Phases
A and B are done, and phase C (PLAN P15) was training when this review was asked for. It assesses the work from first
principles, checks the running experiments, reads the literature on the open questions and sets the next steps. Its
decisions were approved on 9 November (section 6).

Run labels used below:
- **`go2_3level_v2`**: the H-JEPA of 28 October, camera only, trained on corpus v2 before the stand-off data.
- **The control** (`go2_3level_p14_control`): the same model, retrained with phase B's stand-off units.
- **`go2_3level_c`**: phase C's model. The control plus the range ring as 8 tokens, a proprioception token (gyro,
  gravity and the 1.5-s applied-command history), a rollout loss at level 2 and stop-gradient heads.
- **Give-way windows**: decision frames with a walker near and ahead (`mover_probe.give_way_windows`).
- **Walkers that do not yield**: they walk up to the robot and wait for it to leave their path.

## Bottom line

1. **Phase C cannot decide the thesis, whichever way its gates come out.** `go2_3level_c` learned its own command
   history instead of the scene: 89% of its latent's variance is set by the command history, 4% by the camera. And each
   of its gates is matched by something that predicts nothing. A fail is not the "clean negative" P15 promised, and a
   pass would be false.
2. **The control's fail stands, for a narrower claim.** Stand-off data alone does not teach a camera-only model with one
   latent vector how walkers react.
3. **The thesis is untested in its simplest form.** No planner has ever run on the world model. The cost, the critic and
   the planner are unbuilt, and the plan placed them behind the hardest prediction problem.
4. **The next steps reverse that order.** Measures that isolate prediction; the planner and its cost, first on the true
   model for a ceiling; then the planner in missions where the model already suffices. The corrected model follows,
   chosen in a fast test bed and not in 25-hour runs.

## 1. The running experiments

| Service | Doing | State on 9 November, 22:00 |
|---|---|---|
| `hjepa-phase-c` | training `go2_3level_c` | step 50,300 of 60,000 at 1.55 s a step, no restarts |
| `phase-c-interim-gates` | scoring its 40k checkpoint on the CPU | on the last gate |
| `phase-c-gates` | scores the final model on the GPU | waiting for the training |

They ran as designed. The design has two faults.

### 1.1 The latent is the command history

One input at a time was permuted across 960 validation frames (16 units of each of five kinds), the others kept. The
figure is the mean squared change of the latent over twice its total variance: 1.0 means the latent is entirely that
input.

| Share of the latent's variance set by | `go2_3level_c`, 40k | `go2_3level_c`, 20k | The control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera image | 0.04 | 0.02 | 1.00 |
| Range ring | 0.11 | 0.11 | none |
| The 1.5-s command history | 0.89 | 0.89 | none |
| Gyro and gravity | 0.00 | 0.00 | none |

- **Mechanism.** The history recorded with frame t+1 holds the command applied over step t. The inverse-dynamics loss
  (weight 100) asks for the action from two consecutive latents, so it is solved by copying. The regulariser asks for a
  spread-out latent and the prediction loss for a predictable one, and the command history is the one input that is
  perfectly predictable from the actions.
- **In the training record.** At step 49,000 the inverse-dynamics loss is 0.168 in the control and 0.0029 here (level
  2: 0.279 and 0.0021). In validation, one step predicted with shuffled actions costs 0.82 against 0.0076 with the true
  ones; the control's figures are 0.105 and 0.040. At level 2 the figures are 1.81 and 0.015, where 2.0 means unrelated
  states: levels 2 and 3 are almost pure action codes.
- **Effects.** Walker localisation went from 0.13 m (the control) to 0.43 m. The last command reads linearly from the
  latent with R2 0.99.
- **Why nothing flagged it.** The health checks ask that predictions be sensitive to actions and that the latent's rank
  hold. A latent that is the action passes both. Neither has an upper limit.

### 1.2 The gates do not isolate prediction

| Gate (bar) | `go2_3level_c`, interim | What predicts nothing scores |
|---|---|---|
| Give-way at 1.6 / 3.2 s (AUC at least 0.80) | val 0.77 / 0.71 at 20k, 0.73 / 0.61 at 40k | a classifier on the command history alone: 0.77 / 0.73. The walker's distance alone: 0.91 / 0.90 on val and 0.87 / 0.83 on transfer, which passes |
| Way clear after a side step, the 12.8-s level (at least 0.75) | 0.77 val, 0.71 transfer | the same readout without the rollout: 0.77 and 0.70 |
| Contact in 0.8 s (at least 0.9) | 0.93 val, 0.80 transfer | "in contact now": 0.92 and 0.80. Of the contacts 0.8 s ahead, 84% are already contacts |
| Own motion at standstill | passes | trivial once the commands are an input |

- The give-way gate scores the size of a predicted contrast against whether the walker responds. Across windows, that
  label follows the present state: 92% of waiting walkers respond to a retreat and 19% of the others. Anything that
  tracks "a walker is near" or "the robot has stood still" ranks them.
- The rise from the control's 0.44 to 0.77 is matched by the command history alone: a robot that has been standing
  still is usually facing a waiting walker. *Corrected by P16's first scores (STATUS P16):* with the command history
  held constant the model still ranks the walkers that respond at 1.6 s (AUC 0.82; the walker's distance alone scores
  0.84), so part of the rise came from the scene. Its predicted reaction is 4-7% of the true one.
- The valid measure was already in `outputs/phase_c/leakage_and_budget.py`: the slope of the predicted reaction on the
  true one. It is between -0.03 and 0.09 in every model scored, `go2_3level_c` included, where 1 is a correct model.
- At the 12.8-s level the rollout adds nothing to the state in any model (the control: 0.75 against 0.73 on val).

### 1.3 What stands

- **`go2_3level_c` is void as a test of the thesis.** Stopping it was approved. Its 40k checkpoint and the checks above
  are its record.
- **The control and `go2_3level_v2` fail honestly** on the slope: they do not predict the reaction. The stand-off data
  did not change that.
- **The data, the labels and the counterfactual replay are sound.** The replay reproduces the recorded walkers exactly,
  and it is what made these checks possible.

`RESULTS.txt`; CPU only).

## 2. From first principles

The thesis is that behaviour comes from planning with a model learned from observation. That needs six things.

| The thesis needs | Where it stands |
|---|---|
| A latent that holds what decisions depend on | Partly. Walkers in view to 0.13 m, and the robot's own motion. All-round clearance is unknown until the ring is fed in properly |
| Futures that depend on the action, over the decision's horizon | The robot's own motion at 0.8 s. Walkers no better than "stays put"; the reaction slope is near 0 |
| A cost and a critic on the latent | Never built |
| A planner | Never built. The code holds no CEM or MPPI (PLAN P5, steps 3-4) |
| Data in which actions are interventions | The explore units (a random command at 25% of plans). The stand-offs are scripted |
| Memory beyond the model's 0.3 s of context | The hand map and visual odometry |

1. **Two of the six are unbuilt, and they are the cheap ones.** The 7 November plan placed the cost, the critic and the
   planner (phase D) behind the give-way gate, the hardest prediction problem in the project. A planner on the model in a
   static maze is where the JEPA planning papers report success, and it has not been tried here.
2. **The objective explains why the reaction is not learned.**
   - Nothing pays for it. The rollout's error is the same whether the walker reacts or not (P15's budget analysis),
     because the reaction is a small part of a few frames.
   - The stand-off actions are scripted from privileged state: the driver retreats when a walker has been waiting on
     it. A model that conditions on the action then learns the average correlation, retreat goes with following. That
     is the leak of 0.2-0.3 that P15 measured.
   - One latent vector for the whole scene leaves the walker a small share of it.
3. **Routing cannot come from this hierarchy.** Each upper state is a function of one frame's latent, and the mazes'
   grey corridors look alike. The map and the router stay, as the system's memory.
4. **Where the line on rules falls** (approved): estimation and memory may be engineered; decisions may not. The measure
   of "few hand-written rules" is the number of tuned constants on the decision path, about 118 today
   (`go2nav/planning`), counted at every merge beside round trips.
5. **The stand-off decision does not need the walker's displacement predicted.** It needs "holding here costs time for
   ever; the side pocket does not". That is a critic's job, and phase B's data hold the outcomes to train one.

## 3. What the literature says on the open questions

Read from abstracts and summaries, not the full texts, except where the repository already relied on a paper.

| Question | Finding | Consequence here |
|---|---|---|
| Why does the model ignore what matters? | A JEPA keeps what is predictable, not what is relevant (Sobal et al. 2022). A follow-up to LeWM names it "physical representation laziness": the regulariser prevents collapse and the latent still misses the physical state; an auxiliary head that shapes the latent fixes it (Zhu et al. 2026). H-JEPA needs inverse-dynamics supervision on varied scenes | Stop-gradient heads gave up the known remedy. A self-supervised one exists: predict the next range scan from the predicted latent, with its gradient |
| How should proprioception enter? | Terver et al.: a frozen visual encoder, a separate proprioception encoder with its own loss term, and a planning cost that sums both. Never one shared latent trained end to end | A latent token and a loss per sensor. No input that is computable from past actions |
| Can logged data answer "what if I retreat"? | Not when the logged robot was reacting. Conditioning on the ego's future overstates its influence on others (Tang et al. 2022) | Randomised actions in the data, and interventions to score against. The counterfactual replay supplies the second |
| Is one vector enough? | A [CLS]-only world model loses about 30 points to patch tokens on PushT (43% against 75%; Sparse Imagination). Causal-JEPA masks whole objects so that prediction needs the interaction | Tokens per sensor at least; patch or object tokens for the camera later |
| How is the horizon reached? | A value shaped into the latent (Destrade et al.). Hierarchy: H-JEPA goes from 18% to 73% on Visual AntMaze, a benchmark that colours the floor so one frame gives the position | A critic before a three-level planner. The hierarchy's result does not carry to mazes that look alike everywhere |
| A deterministic predictor with walkers that draw a patience at random? | It learns the mean of the futures; Terver et al. name this as the limit of the approach | Expect blurred timing. Score decisions, not trajectories |
| Has anyone planned through agents that react, with a JEPA? | Not found. MA-JEPA is multi-agent RL; FactorJEPA predicts without planning | This part is open research. The test bed must be fast and the measures valid, because no recipe exists |

## 4. Bad patterns and rabbit holes

1. **Gates fixed beforehand but never validated.** Setting a bar in advance does not show that the measure tests what
   it claims. The give-way AUC has steered the work since 29 October (P4e): the top-up, the stand-off data and phase C's
   gate. The check that was missing is cheap: what do a scene-blind model, a present-only readout and an action-blind
   model score?
2. **Offline proxies before any closed loop.** In five weeks the model's prediction has driven nothing beyond 0.8-s
   forecasts of the robot's own motion.
3. **The walker that never yields has been the centre since 6 November, and it is a scripted deadlock.**
   - 102 of 171 pinned stand-off units were dead ends that no move resolves (P14).
   - The scripted side step, with privileged geometry, leaves the way clear in 36% of such windows (holding: 12%).
   - The reference is 7 of 80, and 0 of 40 with two walkers. No agent has shown an upper bound.
   - Meanwhile 34 of 120 polite and four-walker reference missions fail with almost no stand-off time. Their records
     read "no route on the map" and hundreds of plans of "planned arrival settling".
4. **A simulator artefact drove a week.** The slide at a zero command caused most of P8's pose losses (31, then 2 with
   the no-slip pass) and contacts (14, then 5). The memory, pose and vacate pieces (P9-P11) analysed those failures.
5. **Rules moving into data and probes.** The stand-off driver has had five revisions, and its side step is the tape
   of the 12.8-s gate. The model can at best recognise when the scripted move works.
6. **One run called decisive.** `go2_3level_c` changed three things at once against its control (the ring, the
   proprioception token, the level-2 rollout loss), against the project's own rule of one change per piece.
7. **Housekeeping.** `feature/phase-b-data` held 29 unpushed commits, all of P14 and P15, on the system drive that has
   hung twice. Phases A to C are stacked unmerged.

**Worth keeping:** the stop rule, the oracles, the range sensor, the no-slip pass, reactive walkers, the held-out
family C, and above all the counterfactual replay.

## 5. Next steps

| # | Step | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Push `feature/phase-b-data`. Record `go2_3level_c` as void for the thesis | minutes |
| 2 | **Measures that isolate prediction** (PLAN P16). Every score beside its nulls and its privileged ceiling; the reaction read by its slope inside strata the nulls cannot separate; decisions read as the advantage of one tape over another in the same state; a permutation check in validation | about a day, mostly CPU on cached features |
| 3 | **The planner and its cost, first on the true model** (PLAN P17). The kinematic robot and the walkers' own scripts (`mover_probe.branch`) stand in for the world model. It gives the ceiling: what horizon or critic the stand-off needs with perfect prediction, and an upper bound for the suites with walkers that do not yield | 1-2 days, CPU |
| 4 | **The planner in missions where the model already suffices** (PLAN P18). In place of the selection stages, on static and polite suites, with the control's level 1 for the robot's own motion and the ring for clearance | 2-3 days, about 12 GPU-hours |
| 5 | A fast ring world: step 3's kinematic loop with the analytic ring and no rendering, so that designs are chosen over thousands of missions | about 2 days |
| 6 | The corrected model: a latent token and a loss per sensor, no command history in the encoder, next-scan prediction as an auxiliary with gradient. One change per run | 15-25 GPU-hours a run |
| 7 | A critic on cost-to-go, then data collected by the model's own planner | after steps 3-6 |

- **Suites.** Polite, reactive and four walkers become the primary measure. Walkers that do not yield become a stress
  stratum, read against step 3's upper bound.
- **Stop:** gating on the contrast's AUC; further revisions of the scripted drivers; treating any single run as the
  verdict on the thesis.
- **Order.** Each step is its own piece: its own branch, its measures written in PLAN before scoring, the stop rule, a
  merge before the next.

## 6. Decisions (approved 9 November)

1. `hjepa-phase-c` and `phase-c-gates` stop. Whatever `go2_3level_c`'s gates would read is not the thesis's verdict.
2. `feature/phase-b-data` is pushed.
3. Steps 2, 3 and 4 are the next pieces, in that order.
4. The line on rules: engineered estimation and memory are allowed; decisions are learned.
