# Review, 17 November 2026: the object-token JEPA from the toy to the ring world (P27b, P28)

Written on approval (17 November, "1 approved"): P28 stops after Q4, and this review records what the port showed.
It follows docs/REVIEW_2026-11-16.md (the recipe bench, P27). Results are in docs/dynamic/STATUS.md under P27b and
P28. Branch `feature/ring-tokens`, cut from `feature/recipe-bench`; neither is pushed.

## Verdict

- **On the toy, the object-token JEPA keeps all three capabilities and meets P27's eight bars** on the mean of
  three seeds, once its latent is read by an adequate head. The 16 November review's "seven of eight" came from the
  head, not the recipe.
- **In the ring world it learns the walkers' dynamics and their responses to the robot, but trails M5** [the
  supervised object predictor on the ring tracker's tracks] on position over the first seconds. Its best form (Q3, a
  residual predictor) reaches 0.082 / 0.185 / 0.503 m against M5's 0.033 / 0.097 / 0.338. It passes (a) only at
  6.4 s and (b) at no horizon.
- **What separates them is the short-horizon prior, not what the latent holds.** The ring-world tokens are read to
  1.1 cm. M5 predicts a correction to constant velocity in metres, and its rollout looks the walls up at every step.
  Carrying the velocity prior into the latent (Q4) helped the obstacles' motion and cost the action's effect.
- **Promotion to missions is not justified.** Nothing here beats M5, and M5 already runs in the ring world's
  missions.

## Two readings I gave that were wrong

1. **16 November: "R4 meets seven of eight bars; the miss is hidden kinds."** The miss was mostly the head: P27's
   attention-pooling head read positions to 0.24 px from tokens that hold them to 0.09 px. With an MLP head, R4 meets
   all eight. The hidden-kind analysis of false responses still describes where they fall.
2. **17 November: "in the ring world the latent's precision is the limit."** Same cause: the head read 5 cm from
   tokens that hold 2.3 cm (1.1 cm with Q3). The limit there is the predictor.

**Lesson:** before reading a probe's error as what a latent holds, check that a larger head, trained longer, reads
no better. P27's measures split encoder from predictor (retention against forecast) but assumed the head was not
the bottleneck.

## Other lessons

- **BatchNorm projectors and varying content:** a batch whose share of empty slots differs from another's gets
  other statistics. Keep empty tokens out of BatchNorm, encode a window in one call, and log the objective in
  evaluation mode at the end of training. The toy hid this, since all four objects are always present.
- **The toy's bars were absolute, not relative to motion.** A 1-px retention bar for discs moving 1-3 px a step let
  through a read-out that, scaled to the ring world (5 cm against 8 cm a step), could not win. A toy bar should be
  set as a fraction of a step's motion.
- **A latent velocity prior trades capabilities.** With the obstacle's own motion carried by the prior, the
  correction learns less of the action's effect. M5 avoids the trade because its velocity is a measured input in
  metres, not a difference of learned tokens.

## Not tested

- Wall look-ups during the latent rollout (decoding positions and re-encoding the walls each step), the port's known
  handicap at 3.2 and 6.4 s.
- More than one seed in the ring world.
- The confound test, and branches with a paired term, on object tokens.
- T1 [kinds visible] for Q0-Q4.
- Learned estimation: the toy's colour detector and the ring tracker are engineered, so these results are about
  learned dynamics on given objects.
