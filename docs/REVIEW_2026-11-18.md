# Review, 18 November 2026: the recipe bench, P27 to P34b

Written on approval (18 November, "1 approved", after P34b failed): take stock of three days before choosing a
direction. It follows docs/REVIEW_2026-11-16.md (P27) and docs/REVIEW_2026-11-17.md (P27b, P28) and does not repeat
them. Results are in docs/dynamic/STATUS.md under each P, bars in docs/dynamic/PLAN.md. P32-P35 and P34b are on
`feature/object-memory`, not pushed.

## Verdict

- **The dynamics half of the brief is done on the toy.** With the objects given, or learned from pixels where they
  look different, an object-token JEPA [one token per object; LeWM's predictor with a residual (Q3); read by a
  per-token MLP head (Q0)] keeps all three capabilities and meets P27's eight bars on three seeds.
- **On decisions the toy's task shows little either way.** Perfect foresight adds only 4.5 points over constant
  velocity there. The JEPA sits between them (53.5% of tasks done without contact, constant velocity 50.5%, the
  simulator 55.0%) and clearly ahead of Oracle-S [supervised, on true positions]. But it touches obstacles more than
  twice as often, and all of the extra contacts come in tasks with an obstacle that ignores the agent.
- **Two kinds of hidden information remain, and the prediction loss taught neither:**
  - which object is which when they look alike (P31 on the ring world's scans; P33 and P35 on a hard toy);
  - an obstacle's temperament, which only its earlier reactions show (P32). The contacts above suggest this is also
    what costs decisions.
- **The planner cannot buy the contacts back honestly.** Margins from each forecaster's own errors froze it (P34b):
  rabbit hole 4 of the 14 November review, now on the toy.
- **One measurement error is corrected here:** P34's references ran on other tasks than the JEPA. Every verdict
  stands, but the JEPA's lead over constant velocity is no longer clear of noise.

- **The task has little room:** perfect foresight is worth 4.5 points. The JEPA's lead over constant velocity is not
  clear of zero; over Oracle-S it is.
- **The JEPA's extra contacts come only with indifferent obstacles.** The difference between the two groups is +3.0
  points of tasks touched [+0.4, +5.9]. Oracle-S shows no such pattern.
- **Reading** (not proven, since which obstacle was touched is not recorded): P27 found the JEPA gives part of a
  reaction to every obstacle the agent approaches, right on average and false for the indifferent third. Expecting a
  give-way that never comes, the planner sometimes passes too close. Oracle-S hardly reacts at a distance, so it does
  not make this mistake.
- So the weakest capability, action → obstacle under hidden kinds, is also the one that costs decisions here. The
  contacts, which separate the forecasters clearly, say more than success, which barely can.

## What the failures have in common

1. **A prediction loss settles on whatever is easiest to predict.**
   - P35: a keypoint resting on empty floor is perfectly predictable, so joint training lowered the world model's loss
     without binding (19.8% found against its start's 26.7%).
   - P32: an obstacle's kind matters only when the agent is near it, about a fifth of frames, so knowing it barely
     lowers the loss, and the memory was not used.
   - SIGReg keeps the tokens from collapsing; it does not stop the locator or the memory settling on easy structure.
     What we want learned has to be something the loss cannot avoid.
2. **Appearance did the binding.** In P30 each keypoint channel locked onto one colour. With identical obstacles
   (P33) or alike walker arcs (P31), nothing tells the K channels apart, and they pile up, die or drift. The design
   (K separate channels, each a softmax over the whole image) needs a difference between objects that is not there.
   The separation penalties (P30, P33) and the world model's loss (P35) were patches on that design.
3. **A toy pass does not carry when the toy lacks the target's difficulty.** P30 passed on coloured discs; P31 then
   failed on scans, in 40-minute runs. P33's hard toy reproduced the failure in minutes and is now the faster place
   to work on estimation.
4. **Honest uncertainty in an open-loop cost freezes the planner** (P34b). The planner replans every step yet charges
   each 12-step tape as if it could not react. The more honest the margin, the less it moves: constant velocity's
   21-px margin left it 11.5%. No gain in accuracy cures this while the uncertainty is real (the 14 November review).
5. **The forecast's worth on decisions is bounded by the task.** On P34's tasks the planner and the 28-step clock
   decide most outcomes, as in the ring world. P27's measures stay the fast yardstick; P34's contacts are the check.

## Two corrections

1. **P34's references ran on other tasks than the JEPA.** Tasks are drawn from (seed, batch start). Constant velocity,
   Oracle-S and the simulator ran at 50 tasks a batch; the JEPA, after a speed change, at 100, which draws other
   tasks (the obstacles' kinds agree in 3% of them, chance). STATUS called P34's intervals paired; they were not.
   P34b's runs were all at 100, so its forecasters share the JEPA's tasks, but its bar took the simulator from the
   other draw. The three references were rerun on the JEPA's tasks (`outputs/toy_planning/*_paired.json`; no
   training, nothing else changed, 2 minutes):

   | Success without contact (contacts a task) | P34 as reported | On the JEPA's tasks |
   |---|---|---|
   | Constant velocity | 48.7% (0.034) | 50.5% (0.028) |
   | Oracle-S | 47.6% (0.045) | 48.4% (0.036) |
   | JEPA (Q3) | 53.5% (0.064) | 53.5% (0.064) |
   | Simulator | 56.9% (0.001) | 55.0% (0.008) |
   | P34's success bar | 52.8% | 52.8% |
   | P34b's success bar (constant velocity with its margin, 11.5%) | 34.2% | 33.2% |

   Every verdict stands: P34's success bar passes, its contacts bar fails by more, and P34b fails (with margins the
   JEPA still leads constant velocity by 14.1 points [+11.2, +17.0] and Oracle-S by 5.1 [+2.0, +8.2]). What changes:
   the JEPA's lead over constant velocity is 3.0 points [-1.0, +7.0], not 4.8 [+0.4, +9.5], and "58% of what perfect
   foresight adds" is not supported.
2. **"Oracle-S forecasts better on P27's measures" (STATUS P34) was wrong.** On T2 at k = 8 the JEPA (Q3, seed 0) is
   level on the agent (0.43 against 0.41 px) and better on obstacle progression (bounce windows 0.68 against 1.25 px)
   and on response error (0.46 against 0.59 of zero-response); Oracle-S has fewer false responses (4.9% against 9.0%).
   P34b's calibration agrees: the JEPA's gap errors are smaller at every step (10.3 against 11.8 px at 12). Oracle-S
   is 4,000 steps of an MLP, a reference, not a ceiling (as the 16 November review said).

Both are corrected in STATUS.

## Against the brief's rules

- Every run with its measures took 45 minutes or less, except P30's seed 0 (48 minutes; the trainer then cached the
  locator's positions, and the next seeds took 26).
- One change a rung; no rung tuned more than twice. P35 stopped after one of its two rounds, P34b ran its one.
- One seed a rung; three where a rung looked like passing (R4, Q3 on Q0's head, P30's stage 2).
- Each experiment named its capability beforehand; bars went into PLAN before any run, results into STATUS.
- Nothing imports go2nav/research.py. The untracked docs/REVIEW_2026-11-15_repository.md is untouched.
- The stage gates saved runs: P31's stage 2 ran only on your override, and P33's never ran.
- Mistakes of mine: the least-total-distance join in P31 (against PLAN; corrected after round 1), the forecaster bug
  that applied the head twice (caught by a crash, fixed with a test), and the two corrections above. The first of
  those had a common cause: a default changed for speed after the references had run. A comparison should now
  check that its runs share their tasks.

## Not tested

- **Q3 over the blob tracker on the hard toy.** P33's stage 2 never ran, so it is not known whether the dynamics cope
  with 1-3 identical obstacles under bars even when they are tracked by hand.
- The paired branch term [the error of the predicted difference between two action tapes' futures, on the exact
  branches] and the confounded data, on object tokens.
- T1 [kinds visible] for Q0-Q4 and P30-P35; more than one seed for P31-P35.
- Wall look-ups in the ring world's latent rollout.
- A planner that values reacting later (contingency planning, or P22b's learned value) on the toy.
- Which obstacle each contact in P34 was with.
