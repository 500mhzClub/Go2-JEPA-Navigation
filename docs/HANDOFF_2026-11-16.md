## 1. The goal

The README asks: when do JEPA world-model representations carry decision-relevant information for robot navigation,
beyond kinematic prediction, reactive control and explicit memory? On 16 November the user narrowed what to work on.
Use toy worlds to iterate quickly on a **learning recipe**. The recipe must keep three things in a JEPA's predicted
latents:
1. **Action sensitivity:** what the agent's own actions do.
2. **Action → obstacle:** how the agent's actions change what dynamic obstacles do.
3. **Obstacle progression:** how dynamic obstacles move on their own.

Learned heads may read predicted latents into physical quantities. Baselines are fine as cheap references. Do not
spend days re-proving what the Go2 experiments already showed (section 3).

- **The toy had no interaction.** The white balls ignore the red one and pass through it, and the task never needs

- **The slots never bound.** Discs of 3 px under an RGB reconstruction loss are dominated by the background.
- **The runs were too slow to iterate on:**
  - 303,000-step runs were used;

  - an attestation framework ran to 5,000 lines;

## 5. The diagnosis: what a recipe has to fix

| Capability | State of the evidence | The question for the recipe |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Action sensitivity | Solved, even too well: JEPAs keep their own motion | Keep it, without it crowding out the scene |
| 3. Obstacle progression | JEPA latents drop independent movers. Supervised object models keep them. | Which latent structure and loss make a JEPA keep and predict movers? Spatial tokens, object tokens, motion-weighted loss, longer rollouts. |
| 2. Action → obstacle | No JEPA has shown it. M5's version is mostly spurious, from policy-confounded data. | Do exact counterfactual branches and a paired latent loss give real responses and few false ones? |

Every measure must split the predictor's failure from the encoder's: apply the same head to the encoded true future
(what the latent can hold) and to the predicted future (what the predictor carries). This split was missing in the
earlier work.

## 6. Proposed: P27, a recipe bench on reactive balls

Branch `feature/recipe-bench`, cut from main after the two merges. Copy into it:
- `go2nav/sim/balls.py`, `go2nav/data/balls.py`, `go2nav/training/balls.py` and `tests/unit/test_balls.py` from
  `origin/feature/second-world`;
- `go2nav/eval/paired_forecast_metrics.py` from `origin/feature/p25-followthrough`.

**Time box:** about 1 day to build, 2-3 days of rungs. Every run, measures included, takes **45 minutes or less**.

### 6.1 The world (`sim.reactive_balls`; reuse `sim.balls`'s renderer)
- **Frames:** 64 x 64 RGB.
- **The agent:** a red disc (radius 3); an action is its displacement (dx, dy) of up to 3 px.
- **Obstacles:** K = 3, each a distinct colour (green, blue, yellow), so each keeps its identity in heads and
  measures.
- **On their own,** obstacles move at 0.5-2 px a step and bounce off the walls.
- **Their reaction to the agent,** a kind drawn per episode:
  - indifferent (Go2's walkers that do not yield);
  - yielding: pushed away while the agent is within R = 12 px, then back to cruising;
  - pausing (polite walkers): stops while the agent is within R and resumes after a hidden delay of 5-15 steps.
- **Contact:** the agent pushes any obstacle it touches along the contact normal, a direct physical effect.
- **Two variants:** T1, kinds visible (a ring drawn round the disc), the sanity check; T2, kinds hidden, the main
  one, as with Go2's walkers.
- **Data:** 2,000 training and 200 validation episodes of 100 steps, under two policies:
  - *interactive random:* about half the time the agent heads for a random obstacle, with noise, independent of the
    obstacles' kinds and state;
  - *confounded:* a scripted avoider that slows and turns as obstacles come near, like Go2's planner-driven data.
    It is used only for the confound test.
- **Exact branches:** at sampled moments, copy the full state (kinds, timers, randomness) and run 4 alternative 16-step
  tapes: hold, approach, retreat and random. These are the true counterfactual frames and states, cheap in numpy.

### 6.2 The head and the measures (`eval.recipe_bench`; one JSON row per run, under 2 minutes)

**The head.**
- Its input is the frozen JEPA latent, with the gradient stopped. Its outputs are the agent's position and each
  obstacle's position and visibility.
- It is an MLP for a [CLS] latent, or attention pooling for tokens.
- It is trained after the JEPA, on encoded training frames.
- It is applied three ways: to the encoded current frame, to the **encoded true future** (retention) and to the
  **predicted future** (forecast).

**The measures,** at k = 1, 4, 8 and 16 steps:

| Measure | What it is |
|---|---|
| E, ego | The agent's error from the forecast. The *action-shuffle ratio*: the error with shuffled actions over the error with the true ones. |
| X, progression | An obstacle's error where the agent stays more than 2R away, split into bounce and no-bounce windows. Against constant velocity and the oracle. |
| A, action → obstacle | On branch pairs: the true response (the obstacle under tape A less under tape B) against the predicted one (through the head). Response error against zero-response (`paired_forecast_metrics`). Slope with a bootstrap interval. False-response rate (true response under 0.5 px, predicted over 1.5 px). Split by kind, and by contact against reaction at a distance. |
| R, retention | The head on the encoded true future: what the latent can hold at all. |

**References,** without a JEPA, each minutes to compute:
- constant velocity and hold, on true states;
- **Oracle-S:** a supervised object predictor on true positions, velocities and actions, with the same rollout. It
  is M5's analogue and the attainable level.

Report every recipe as its share of the gain Oracle-S makes over constant velocity (on X) or over zero (on A).

### 6.3 The recipe ladder: one change a rung, one seed, three seeds only where a rung looks like passing

| Rung | Recipe | Tests | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | LeWM [CLS] (`training.balls` L0, with an 8-step rollout loss) | The known negative, one short run only | exists; the rollout loss is a small change |
| R1 | **Patch-token JEPA:** every 8 x 8 patch token predicted. `LeWMConfig(grid=(8, 8))` already gives one token a patch with block-causal AdaLN and SIGReg per token. | Can spatial tokens keep small movers (R, X)? Never run in this project (P4b pooled patches to 3 x 4; P23 item 6, the patch baseline, has no result). | a config |
| R2 | R1 with a motion-weighted loss: each token's error weighted by its target's change (the idea of VideoSAUR's motion target) | Can movers be kept when R1 drops them? | small |
| R3 | R1 (or R2) trained on **branches:** each history with 2 tapes, loss on both futures' latents, and a paired term ((pred_A - pred_B) against (z_A - z_B)) | Capability 2 and false responses; with and without the confounded data | new sampler |
| R4 | **Object-token JEPA:** an engineered colour detector places the objects; each token embeds a 16 x 16 crop and its position; the predictor runs over object tokens, the agent token and the action; the targets are the crops' future embeddings (C-JEPA masking optional) | "Estimation engineered, dynamics learned", in latent space; the form that ports to the ring tracker's objects | new |
| R5 | The best of R1-R4 with longer history (8-16 frames) or a recurrent state token | The hidden kinds and delays of T2 | small |

**How to read a failure:**
- R fails: the encoder drops the object. Try R2, or tokens per object.
- R passes but X fails: the predictor. Try a longer rollout loss and more history.
- A gives false responses: confounding. Try R3.
- A is near zero: the action is not reaching the obstacles' tokens. Check the conditioning; use contact-rich data.

Two rounds at most on any rung (docs/REVIEW_2026-11-14, rabbit hole 3); then the next rung.

### 6.4 Bars (write them into PLAN before the first run; T2, 3 seeds, k = 8)
- **E:** agent error at most 1 px; action-shuffle ratio at least 5.
- **X:** obstacle error at most half constant velocity's on bounce windows, and no more than 0.5 px above it on the
  others.
- **A:**
  - response error at most half zero-response's on responsive pairs;
  - false responses on at most 10% of non-responsive pairs;
  - slope interval above 0.5.
- **R:** retention error at most 1 px for every object.
- A recipe that passes is promoted. If none passes after R5, write a review of what each rung showed; do not add
  rungs.

**Compute:**
- R0-R3 at about 64 tokens x 4 frames, 20-30k steps at batch 64-128, about 20-40 minutes each.
- At most two GPU processes at once.
- Building: world, branches, head, measures and Oracle-S, about a day.
- Check that the measures separate Oracle-S from constant velocity and zero **before** any JEPA run (does the world
  have measurable reactions?).

## 8. Traps

- **Python:** the `genesis_rocm_0_4_6_v1` virtual environment under LeWMQuad-v3's `.generated/venvs/` (the chain scripts
  in outputs/genesis and outputs/balls name it in full).
  - Tests need `PYTEST_DISABLE_PLUGIN_AUTOLOAD=1` (a ROS pytest plugin breaks collection).
  - Do not run scripts from the scratchpad's root: a `profile.py` there shadows the standard library. Use a
    subfolder.
- **GPU:** the R9700 is torch's `cuda:0`; keep under 85% of its memory (it drives the desktop).
  - Six trainers at once hung it on 15 November and needed a reboot. Run at most two GPU processes, or several small
    models in one process.
  - Pause a job with `systemctl --user stop`, never a freeze. A transient `systemd-run` unit disappears on stop;
    relaunch it with `systemd-run`.
  - Train on the GPU only.
- **ROCm numerics:**
  - LayerNorm affine gradients are wrong on this build: use `models.norm.LayerNorm`.
  - bf16 autocast faulted P23's slots: use float32.
  - Check GPU against CPU gradients once per new architecture, with dropout off. Dropout masks differ between
    devices; parameters just before a BatchNorm have gradients near zero, so ignore those.
  - Do **not** chase float64 agreement of recursive rollouts.
- **LeWM's BatchNorm projectors:** their running statistics gave 10-25 times the validation error of batch statistics
  (follow-through, IMPLEMENTATION_2026-11-15). Recompute them on training windows (cumulative average) before any
  evaluation.
