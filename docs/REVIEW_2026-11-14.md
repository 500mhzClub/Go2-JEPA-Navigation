# Review, 14 November 2026: are we chasing our tail?

Asked for on 14 November, after N5 failed: re-review the approach throughout, name the rabbit holes, suggest
alternatives. Approved the same day ("Approved"): steps 1-3 below now, step 4 as the next piece whatever they show.

## Verdict

Yes. Since the morning of 12 November every turn has gone the same way: the model fails the planner bar on four walkers
that do not yield, a new probe explains why, the data or the rule is patched, the model is retrained, and it fails
again. Six turns (several futures, the expected cost, the memory, the branches, the decision rule, the planner's own
states), about 130 suites in three days. Each probe found something real. The loop began with a wrong turn, and the
yardstick keeps us inside it.

## Rabbit holes

**1. The best model was left behind.** Several futures were approved at 10:28 on 12 November, on one partial seed of
M4 (one learned future) under the old rule. M5 passed (a) at 14:14 the same day and has never run in a mission.

| Likeliest future, median error (m) | 1.6 s | 3.2 s | 6.4 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| The ring tracker's constant velocity | 0.054 | 0.272 | 0.776 |
| M5 (one future, a correction to constant velocity) | **0.033** | **0.101** | **0.325** |
| N4 (several futures) | 0.065 | 0.165 | 0.424 |

N4 fails (a) at every horizon, and at 1.6 s, the range the robot acts on, it is worse than constant velocity. Several
futures also made the planner price probabilities, which brought the freezing and two days of rule work (items 12 and
13). That rule now helps models of one future too (the tracker 12.5 to 16.25 round trips), so the cell that matters
most is empty: M5 under it.

**2. The stress suite became the yardstick again.** The review of 9 November called the walker that never yields "a
scripted deadlock" and made it a stress stratum. The second review of 11 November made four of them the primary suite
because they had the most room, while noting that the room is "mostly a walker sealing the one route of a tree maze"
(in the loops-and-rooms family a walker model is worth 2-3 round trips). Even perfect predictions time out in about 11
of 40 of those missions; the tracker's own seeds span 12-20 round trips, so N5's gain of 0.75 over N4 cannot be seen.
Missed on the way: the learned models lose on the easier suites as well (polite 74-75 against the tracker's 78,
reactive 69-73 against 74, 1-2 walkers that do not yield 38-49 against 59-62; several-futures models under the old
rule).

**3. Planner tuning came back under another name.** On 11 November: "the planner is not worked on further". Since then:
eight new switches in `planning.sampling`, 72 sensitivity suites, and a walker price chosen on seed 0 of the suite it
is scored on (realistic errors 26 there, 19-21 on the others). Nothing has merged since, so the count of decision-path
constants promised at each merge was never reported; P22 grew from item 7 to item 13 without closing.

**4. Costing an open-loop future makes honest uncertainty freeze the robot.** The robot acts for 0.4 s and replans, yet
each tape is charged for the contacts 6.4 s out as if it could not react. At 6.4 s the best model errs 0.33 m against
a floor of 8 cm, so a calibrated spread there puts some chance of contact on nearly every tape that moves: the
freezing-robot problem (Trautman and Krause 2010). The tracker escapes it by being confidently wrong: more round trips,
paid for in contacts. No gain in accuracy cures this while the uncertainty is real.

**5. Probes multiplied; fixes stayed local.** Tape optimism, the stand-off tables, traces, mixtures, the calibration,
the term split, the oracles; each fix one more share of data or one more switch. The probes read each model at its own
states, so they explain a model but cannot rank two; the ranking fell to missions, slow and noisy.

**6. The thesis is off the critical path.** P22 is supervised trajectory prediction on engineered tracks (the second
review's words). Since 11 November the self-supervised side has had three slot runs: s0, s1 and s2 all land between
78% and 86%, and the first is still the best overall. P24 (the predictor on Genesis's tracks) could start once M5
passed (a); the second world (bouncing balls) was approved and never started.

**A correction to the day's oracle.** `dice:8` copies each walker's pause timer and chosen next cell, so it is not "the
best any model could do": if it freezes, the rule is to blame; if it passes, it says nothing about models.

## What the evidence supports

- The planner works when the futures are sharp and right: perfect predictions 29.25 round trips, realistic errors
  21.5, no contact stops.
- Learned dynamics beat constant velocity offline by two to three times (M5).
- The reaction to the robot is partly learned (slope about 0.4; bar 0.5).
- Branched data removes part of the confound with the planner's tapes.
- Self-supervised slots bind 85% of the walkers in view (untrained: 21%).
- The ring world with exact replays is the project's best tool.

## Plan (approved)

| # | Step | Why | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stop the several-futures line; freeze the rule as it stands, its constants counted | ends the loop | minutes |
| 2 | M5 under the frozen rule: four walkers that do not yield, seeds 0-3; 1-2 walkers of each kind, seed 0 | fills the empty cell; decides P22's (c) | about 5 h of GPU inference |
| 3 | A bank of planning moments fixed once: each tape's true cost from exact replays; a model's score is the true cost of the tape it picks | paired, quiet, minutes a model; missions only at milestones, on a mixed suite | about half a day of CPU |
| 4 | A learned value of the state: the model prices only the next 1.6-3.2 s, where it is accurate; a value learnt from the planner's own missions prices the rest | takes over the hand terms (the distance field, the sealed cost, the walker price, the way out, the still future); learnt from a robot that replans, it values reacting later, which cures the freezing at its root. LeCun's configurable critic; TD-MPC2's value; on the plan since 9 November and deferred each time | about a day to build; GPU training |
| 5 | Back to the thesis: P24 offline on Genesis's tracks, then the second world | generality and the JEPA claim | as planned |

Contingency planning (tapes share their first 0.8 s, then take the best continuation in each future; Hardy and
Campbell 2013) is the hand-built fallback for step 4. For vision, s0 is the slot model until the join can say whether
85% is enough. Written out in PLAN P22 item 14.
