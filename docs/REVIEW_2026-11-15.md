# Review, 15 November 2026: the Genesis port against the project's goals

Asked for on 15 November, after the fourth smoke of P24's stage 2: re-evaluate the Genesis port from first principles,
make sure we are not chasing heuristics or the wrong thing, and judge it against the research project's stated goals.

## Verdict

Stage 1 did what was asked of P24. Stage 2 became the thing the review of 14 November named rabbit hole 3, planner
tuning under another name:
- four smoke rounds, each patching how P17's planner fits the Genesis harness;
- each round added a rule or a constant;
- nothing in them measured M5 or the JEPA claim.

Stop stage 2 here, close P24 on stage 1, and go back to the thesis by the plan already approved.

## The goals, as stated

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| README | "When do JEPA world-model representations carry decision-relevant information for robot navigation, beyond kinematic prediction, reactive control and explicit memory?" All controllers share one harness; only the forecast differs. |
| PLAN, dynamic phase | Which parts of the harness break with moving obstacles, and which can a world model take over? |
| PLAN P20-P23 (11 November) | "Can a model learned from observation predict what the walkers will do ... well enough that planning on it beats planning without it?" The decision is P18's cost, and "the planner is not worked on further". The Genesis piece was to be "planned on through P18's cost". |
| Review of 14 November, step 5 (approved) | "Back to the thesis: P24 offline on Genesis's tracks, then the second world", for "generality and the JEPA claim". |
| The user's standing rule | A self-supervised world model and a planner, with as few hand-written decision rules as possible. Engineered estimation and memory are allowed; decisions come from learning or planning. |

## What P24 did, against them

1. **Stage 1, offline, met step 5.** On 400 Genesis units, through the tracker M5 was trained on, M5 beats constant
   velocity at every horizon (0.028, 0.074 and 0.251 m against 0.047, 0.192 and 0.671). It falls short of half for walkers
   held up by the robot. Learned object dynamics carry from the ring world into Genesis.
2. **Stage 2a** is a useful fact for any later planner: the kinematic model follows the Go2 to 2.4 cm at 3.2 s.
3. **Stage 2 itself changed the decision and the predictor at once,** against the README's method (one harness, only
   the forecast differs) and the 11 November plan (P18's cost fixed). Even a passing suite would have measured
   mostly the planner.
4. **Its four smoke rounds were about the harness's contract, not about walkers.**
   - The route's waypoint is a look-ahead point under 0.4 m away.
   - The reflex sweeps a 0.45-m disk where P17 keeps 8 cm.
   - The Go2 does not execute closing commands under 0.1 m/s or reverse pulses.
   - The arrival is a 2-cm disk held for a second.
   - Frontier waypoints are left unchanged while the robot holds.
   - P18 spent three rounds of fixes on exactly these. Each of our rounds added another: an approach floor, terminal
     pulses, a hand-over radius, the route's end as the target, and next the reflex look-ahead.

## Why it went wrong

- P24's text (from the second review of 11 November) prescribed P17's planner in missions. I followed it without
  checking it against the plan it contradicted, or against step 5's narrower scope.
- P17's planner was built for a known layout, exactly executed commands and a fixed target. The harness gives none of
  these; porting it is a new planner, not a swap.
- The smoke was the only check, so each failure invited a patch: the loop of 14 November in a new place.

## Where the thesis stands

- The JEPA representations in Genesis (C5's and C7's latents) hold little of the walkers (P16: a reaction slope of
  0.03-0.07). The ring JEPA failed (P21).
- What predicts walkers well is M5: trajectory prediction on engineered tracks, not a JEPA. The second review called
  it the ceiling for what object-level dynamics can give a planner.
- The self-supervised object path is stalled: three slot runs bind 78-86% of walkers (bar 90%).
- The second world (bouncing balls) was approved and never started.
- In the ring world the decision, not the prediction, limits the hard suite (M5 level with the honest oracle). A
  learnt value did not help (P22b).
