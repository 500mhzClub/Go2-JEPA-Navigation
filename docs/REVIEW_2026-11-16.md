# Review, 16 November 2026: the recipe bench (P27)

Written because the plan requires it: no recipe passed P27's bars as written after R5 (docs/dynamic/PLAN.md P27
item 4). Results are in docs/dynamic/STATUS.md, the sections headed P27. Branch `feature/recipe-bench`.

## Verdict

The bench did what it was built for. In one day it separated the recipes clearly:
- **Object tokens (R4) keep all three capabilities in predicted latents.** On T2 [kinds hidden] they meet seven of
  the eight bars on the mean of three seeds, and are level with the supervised reference or better.
- **Global and patch latents (R0-R3) do not.** The encoder can be made to hold every disc; no predictor over those
  latents carried them one step ahead.
- **The one bar R4 misses, false responses (11.2% against 10%), is located:** obstacles of hidden kind that the agent
  comes near. A longer history (R5) did not resolve it.

By the rule, nothing is promoted without your decision (below).

## What each rung showed

| Rung | Finding |
|---|---|
| References | The measures separate Oracle-S from constant velocity and zero-response. Oracle-S passes every bar on T1 and misses A's error bar on T2: with the kinds hidden and 3 frames of history, half of zero-response's error was not attainable by it. |
| R0 | The known negative again: the latent keeps the agent and one obstacle, and drops two. |
| R1 | Spatial tokens make the encoder keep every disc (1.4-3.1 px). The predictor loses them all, the agent included. A head trained on the predictions themselves cannot find them either. |
| R2 | Weighting each token by its patch's pixel change brings retention to about 1 px and part of the agent's forecast back. Obstacles' forecasts stay at chance. |
| R3 | Branches and a paired term did not make a patch-token predictor carry obstacles. Unweighted, they collapsed the encoder; weighted, the encoder held and the predictor carried nothing. |
| R4 | With the objects placed by an engineered detector and one token an object, the same LeWM predictor and loss learn the agent's motion, the walls, and the reactions. 6,000 steps were too few; 36,000 took 25 minutes. |
| R5 | 16 frames of history did not improve reactions at a distance (0.64 against 0.65 of zero-response). The agent is near an obstacle in 20% of frames, so the history seldom shows a kind. |

## Why R4 misses the false-response bar

| Quiet pairs (true response under 0.5 px), k = 8 | Pairs | R4, T2 | Oracle-S, T2 | R4, T1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The agent never within the reaction radius | 12,716 | 2.3% | 1.8% | 13.4% |
| Within it in one tape only, no contact | 888 | 61% | 13% | 23% |
| Within it in both tapes, no contact | 957 | 52% | 21% | 31% |
| A contact in either tape | 294 | 68% | 62% | 58% |
| All | 14,855 | 10.3% | 4.9% | 16.0% |

(R4's seed 0.)
- **On T2 it is the hidden kind.** An indifferent obstacle the agent approaches looks, for 3 frames, like one that
  will yield or pause. A model with one predicted future gives part of a reaction to all of them. That is right on
  average (slope 0.51 at a distance) and false for the indifferent third.
- Oracle-S avoids it by hardly reacting at a distance (slope 0.26). The bar on response error and the bar on false
  responses pull against each other here.
- **On T1 the kinds are used** (indifferent obstacles: 59% false falls to 16%; slope at a distance 0.70), but T1 is
  less precise everywhere (13% false far from the agent). Why the rings cost precision is not known.
- Seeds differ in precision: 2.3% (seed 0) and 6.9% (seed 2) false far from the agent.

## What the bench did not test

- **The confound test** (R3 with the scripted avoider's data). No patch-token predictor carried obstacles, so there
  was nothing to confound. The data exist (outputs/data/reactive_balls/confounded).
- **Branches and the paired term on object tokens.** R3 was defined on R1 or R2.
- **Estimation.** R4's detector knows the four colours. Its tokens' positions are inputs, so R passes by
  construction; R4 tests learned dynamics, not learned perception.
- **Why T1 reads worse than T2** for every recipe.
- **More than one seed** for R0-R3 and R5, and more than 6,000 steps for R1-R3. The patch-token predictor might
  learn with far longer training; at 0.17 s a step that does not fit the 45 minutes.
- Oracle-S is 4,000 steps of an MLP. It is a reference, not a ceiling; R4 beats it on X and A.

## How the day went against the handoff's cautions

- Every run and its measures took 45 minutes or less (R5 was restarted at 30,000 steps to keep to it).
- No rung was tuned more than twice. No attestation or ledger code was written.
- Two things cost time: Oracle-S on this GPU (28 minutes), and running two trainers at once, which runs each at
  under a third of the solo speed.
